# Lantern Wake current beacon rules

New rooms created by normal startup use room schema2 and match schema3/worldVersion1/beaconVersion1. Existing schema1 board and schema2 walking-world matches remain on their original rules; no identities, accepted action IDs, spending reservations or replay records are rewritten. The historical engine notes below describe earlier versions. This section takes precedence for new beacon matches.

The default watch remains six keepers, five crew/one saboteur, one human or all AI. Server-side roster interfaces support4..12 explicit IDs, multiple declared human slots and1..3 saboteurs with a starting crew majority; the current join endpoint still issues spectators only. Rules use actual roster/human flags, not fixed p1 or six-seat authority. See NEXT_FEATURE_INTERFACES.md before adding multiplayer allocation.

Walking is180 units/s, normalized diagonally, collision-limited by floors, props and currently closed doors. Held input expires600ms after its latest accepted sequence. High-level navigation is a finite validated route, not teleportation. Dynamic doors can stop a route. Manual movement or a new intent cancels it. Stops on hide/exit are best effort; if a network failure prevents the stop, manual steering expires while an accepted finite click route can finish. Servers and AI continue independently of the browser.

Crew sight360 and saboteur sight400 are configurable server-side (80..900). Closed doors and solid props occlude vision and interactions. Lights reduce crew/public-camera sight to110 (configurable no greater than normal crew sight); saboteur sight is unaffected. Task/repair/bell range92, elimination70, report130 also require actual line of sight. No client position, action hint or model judgment grants authority.

Death knowledge is explicit. A living keeper learns a fallen identity from local body/attack observation, a personally sampled vitals console, or the next council muster. Otherwise its previous known status remains, or stays unknown; current hidden positions and global death notifications are not sent. Last-known alive is historical knowledge, not confirmation of present life. Council muster discloses the dead roster without remote coordinates or roles. Unfinished tasks redistribute only at muster. A fixed Bridge spectator viewpoint uses its own bounded knowledge and cannot follow hidden deaths or hatch travel; all roles/positions become available only after resolution. Camera observations never become attack eyewitnesses.

Auxiliary sabotage (lights, communications, doors) has a shared20s per-saboteur cooldown, separate from the65s critical cooldown. Lights last45s or end on one crew repair at the Workshop lighting relay. Communications lasts35s or ends on one crew repair at the Bridge radio relay; it blocks all device reads and cancels active sessions. Historical samples are retained and labeled. Council dialogue remains available. A door closes12s; any living keeper can reopen from its accessible face panel, or wait for expiry. At most two doors may be closed across the match. Closing a door never moves someone through a wall; a keeper in a closing doorway may wait for reopening. Council reopens all doors. Auxiliary expiries retain their actual saved deadlines after restart.

One critical failure may be active: power or flooding. Power needs Engine turbine regulator plus Workshop power coupler; flooding needs Galley pump plus Archive pressure valve. Each site is completed by one distinct living crew member, and each crew contributes at most once per failure. The75s default countdown continues during council; reports remain possible, while the emergency bell is unavailable during critical failure. Finishing both sites before the deadline repairs the failure. Missing its exact deadline gives the saboteurs the win. Public repair progress omits contributor identities.

Only living saboteurs may enter linked service hatches while physically visible/in range and off cooldown. Entry stops movement, hides the actor's position from other views, and prevents movement/equipment actions during1.6s travel. Exit is the chosen validated fixture, followed by15s cooldown. Entering council cancels travel back at its original position without refund; terminal phases also cancel it. Only actual entry/exit witnesses (and the actor) receive dated local traces, showing the one observed hatch, never the other end, role or recipient list. No actor or route attribution is globally broadcast.

Consoles require2s of stationary contact and line of sight, with5s personal cooldown after completion/cancellation. Leaving, steering, navigation, death, transit, phase change or communications failure cancels without a new sample. Exact-deadline sampling uses authoritative state settled at that instant; an overdue unreconstructable restored read fails stale instead of backdating present telemetry. Security at Bridge samples Archive/Engine camera fields (at most six visible living keepers per camera; collection limits explicit), subject to lights, floors/props/doors and transit. Archive occupancy counts living non-transit keepers physically inside six rooms, excluding corridors and without identities. Galley vitals samples alive/dead IDs without roles, locations or cause. Records are immutable dated samples, retained only for their reader up to64; no terminal exposes unrestricted state or other readers' sessions.

Field-note is a baseline extension action that logs the actor's own real position/time privately, with5s cooldown and32-note bound. It adds no special role. Interactive-task, strategic-goal, roster, death/ability, panel and replay registration contracts are executable and documented in NEXT_FEATURE_INTERFACES.md. Extra ghost/ability roles and public multiplayer seat allocation are reserved for Group2 and are not silently enabled.

Crew wins by completing all assigned tasks or eliminating all saboteurs by ejection. Saboteurs win at living parity or critical expiry. Votes have the existing unique-plurality rule: ties, winning skip or no votes eject nobody; missing ballots abstain. One emergency per keeper,35s emergency cooldown, discussion30s, voting20s; critical clocks and action cooldowns continue through council. Four-minute unattended roam calls council. Fifteen-minute maximum pauses transparently with no invented winner.

At equal times, critical loss precedes maximum duration, then phase deadlines, system expiry/vent arrival, then device samples. Motion settles only to the next authoritative boundary, and each transition is processed at that original timestamp. New action validation happens after elapsed deadlines. Failed requests and denied input sequences retain their original results across restart. Private replay events preserve canonical IDs/times; beacon snapshots are retained at5s intervals plus resolution, at most256 while preserving the start. Replay and global snapshots are unavailable before ended, including paused matches.

No paid model calls are required for these systems. Local safe-view navigation handles them in both demo and hybrid modes. Council may reference its own dated device facts while distinguishing samples, inferences, dialogue and actual attack witnesses. All existing per-match/durable daily reservations, provider settings and explicit demo fallback remain enforced.

## Historical schema1 engine notes

﻿Integration clarification: a keeper may submit at most2,048 well-formed unique request IDs per match, including rule denials. Old IDs are retained, never evicted into eligibility for a second mutation. After the cap, new IDs are explicitly rejected and retained duplicates stay inert. AI-prefixed IDs and browser UUIDs use the same bounded envelope. The transport persists engine-produced denial/deadline state before returning an error.

The five named exports live in `server/engine/index.mjs`: `createMatch`, `applyAction`, `tick`, `getPlayerView`, `getReplay`. No dependency installation is required. `createMatch` accepts rule overrides directly as `config` (transport passes `config.rules` here), using the exact shared default keys. Internal serializable state has `schemaVersion:1`, `players`, `requests`, `replay`, timestamps, and rules; transport must keep all of it private. Only engine views go to clients and AI. No contract additions are required.

A match begins roaming with six keepers, exactly five crew and one saboteur. The role and room distribution use a cryptographic random secret in production; explicit test seeds reproduce state. Public match IDs do not influence roles. Human mode reserves p1; spectator mode has no human. The secret is never retained.

Movement follows a single map edge. Crew complete their own assigned relay tasks in the task's room. Completed tasks cannot be repeated. Three tasks per crew are assigned by default. On death/ejection, unfinished crew tasks are redistributed round-robin to living crew, preserving task IDs and total task count; completed tasks remain counted. Dead keepers cannot act or vote. This avoids requiring an absent/dead human to finish tasks.

Initial movement/task cooldowns are zero. Initial elimination cooldown is 18 seconds, sabotage 65 seconds, emergency bell 35 seconds. Subsequent movement/task/elimination/sabotage cooldowns are 1.2/4/22/65 seconds. Sabotage can be started remotely and creates a public critical failure in Engine. Two distinct living crew in Engine must each repair once before its 75-second deadline. The saboteur cannot repair. A repaired critical failure remains visible with active:false until another sabotage replaces it.

The saboteur can eliminate a living crew member in the same room. A body ID is `body-<victimId>`. Report requires a locally visible, unreported body. Reporting clears all currently unreported bodies. The bridge emergency bell requires its cooldown, one remaining emergency use (one per keeper by default), and no active critical failure. A report remains possible during critical failure.

Meetings freeze roaming actions. Action cooldowns and the critical countdown continue in wall-clock time. Meeting positions stay where they were; other players' positions remain local observations. Discussion lasts 30 seconds, then voting lasts 20 seconds. Each living keeper can send three messages of at most 280 characters; surrounding whitespace is trimmed, control characters rejected. Each living keeper can vote once for a living keeper (including self) or skip. No early tally occurs. The unique plurality wins: skip plurality, any tie including skip, or no votes means no ejection. Missing voters abstain. Targets stay hidden until tally. The eligible voter list is fixed at the meeting start. After tally the next roaming deadline is four minutes away, and all emergency bell cooldowns are at least 35 seconds from tally. Reports have no bell cooldown.

A missed four-minute roaming deadline calls an automatic meeting, even if browsers are disconnected. Tick processes all elapsed deadlines at their original times, so a delayed call does not extend discussion/voting/critical time. At an exact deadline the transition occurs before any submitted action. Critical failure has precedence at simultaneous deadlines, then maximum duration, then phase transitions. A 15-minute maximum duration pauses the match with a public paused event and no winner; there is no automatic resume action in v1. Paused matches remain available for inspection, replay remains locked, and transports must label the pause truthfully.

Crew win by completing all tasks or ejecting the saboteur. The saboteur wins at living parity or a missed critical deadline. No model or timeout declares a synthetic winner. Ended matches reject further actions.

Safe view keys exactly match CONTRACT.md. `availableActions` contains type names validated against present phase/location/cooldowns. Spectators see public physical positions but no self/private tasks/roles before the end. Players see only local positions, their own role/tasks, aggregate task progress, and public status. No human/AI flag, hidden task assignments, request IDs, rules, seed, repairer list, replay, or pause internals leak into views. Roles appear on `players` only when ended.

The frozen view has no bodies list. A locally visible unreported body appears as an event `{id:bodyId,type:'body',bodyId,playerId,roomId,at}`; AI/UI use `bodyId` to report it. Eyewitnesses receive `{id,type:'witness',at,playerId:eliminatorId,targetId,roomId}` only if alive and physically present at the elimination. Public eliminated events expose only victim ID; no killer or kill room. Task/move/sabotage actor attribution is absent from public events. Dialogue is explicitly untrusted text and must render via textContent. Vote result events contain tally targets. Each view has at most 256 timeline entries. Views and replay are defensive copies.

`getReplay` returns null before ended (including paused), then an array of fully attributed events including actions, task transfers, outcome, and final role reveal. To bound persistent memory, it retains the latest 12,000 replay records; the final role reveal is always included. Public events retain256 records. Requests retain up to2,048 valid envelopes per actor, including rule denials; new IDs then reject explicitly. An identical retained request repeats its original result without executing; different contents under the same ID reject with REQUEST_CONFLICT. IDs are never evicted during a match. This survives JSON persistence. Malformed envelopes are safely denied and never persisted. Accepted wait and persisted rule denials are actual state changes and increment version; a no-op tick or duplicate does not. A late action can also include events from automatic deadline processing. Transport must persist returned state even on denial, and must not expose internal state or raw replay before ended.

Task IDs are opaque deterministic hashes under the private seed; their sequence never reveals which preceding keeper has the hidden role. Untallied ballots stay hidden even when a match pauses during voting. The internal tally marker is excluded from the exact shared meeting shape.

## Optional ghost and ability presets

Classic task transfer remains the default. The separately validated world `features` setting can choose `deathPolicy:'ghost-tasks'` and/or `abilities:true`. Ghosts retain original unfinished crew tasks without transfer, remain dead, use ordinary world collisions, and never participate in living council, repair, reports or parity. Fallen chat is delivered only to authenticated dead actors. Harbor Ward and Signal Echo use separate ability metadata, authoritative expiry and persisted cooldowns.
